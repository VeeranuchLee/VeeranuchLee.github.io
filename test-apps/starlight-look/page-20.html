Other stars have planetsAnd we found them without ever seeing one

When a planet passes in front of its star, it blocks a tiny bit of the light. Not enough to see. Just enough to measure.

Ari: “But Dot… how can we know a planet is there if we can’t even see it?”
Dot: “New mission accepted.”

A planet crossing a star is called a transit.
A Jupiter-size planet blocks about 1% of the light. An Earth-size one, about 0.01%.
NASA's Kepler (launched 2009) found more than 2,600 planets this way.
NASA's TESS (launched 2018) looks at stars near us.
Over 6,000 planets around other stars are now known.
