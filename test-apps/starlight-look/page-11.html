Where stars come fromCold clouds of gas and dust

Stars are not put in the sky. They are made — out of huge, cold, dark clouds drifting between the stars that are already there.

Dot: “Drag your finger through the cloud. Pull the dust together, and watch what gravity starts doing on its own.”

A glowing cloud of gas and dust is called a nebula.
Star-making clouds are very cold: about −260°C.
They are about 99% gas (mostly hydrogen) and 1% dust.
The Orion Nebula is a star nursery about 1,350 light-years away.
Our Sun was born in a cloud like this about 4.6 billion years ago.
