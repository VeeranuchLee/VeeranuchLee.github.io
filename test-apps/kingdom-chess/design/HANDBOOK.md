## Welcome

Welcome to Kingdom Chess. Every kingdom looks different, but every match uses the same rules as a real chessboard. Dawn moves first. Your aim is to checkmate the other king: place the king in danger with no safe way out.

Take your time. Chess is not a race. Look, wonder, choose, and learn from the next position.

## The story of Princess Unicorn: The Bridge of Shared Light

One misty morning, the rainbow bridge above Prism Vale lost three of its colours. Queen Celestia Brightmane could have ordered everyone to search at once, but she first asked Sage Lumen to study the paths. The wise bishop noticed that each missing colour had drifted along a diagonal beam.

Rainbow Keep watched the straight roads. Comet the unicorn leapt over a hedge and found a blue spark hiding in the dew. The Gleam Scouts moved forward together, one careful step at a time, passing a golden spark from hoof to hand. King Rowan Starcrown stayed where he could see the whole vale and made sure nobody searched alone.

At last, Celestia reached the hilltop. She could travel straight down the path or diagonally across the clover, so she gathered the three sparks quickly. Yet the colours would not join until every helper placed a light beside another.

The bridge blazed back to life—not because one hero had been fastest, but because each friend had moved in a different, useful way. Celestia smiled. “A kingdom shines brightest when every piece has a purpose.”

That evening, the two sides set out a chessboard beneath the restored rainbow. They played slowly, thanked each other for clever moves, and crossed the bridge together when the game was done.

## The story of Princess Mermaid: The Quiet Current

A baby turtle had become lost beyond the singing reef. Queen Nerina Pearlheart wanted to rush after it, but King Maris Tidekeeper raised one calm hand. The tide was turning, and a hurried swimmer could be carried farther away.

Coralie climbed to a coral lookout and traced diagonal currents through the clear water. Shellwatch Tower guarded the straight channel home. Ripple the sea-horse darted around a rock in a surprising bent path and discovered a trail of tiny bubbles. The Bubble Pages followed one by one, never crowding the narrow reef.

Nerina saw the whole pattern. She swam straight through the lagoon, then diagonally between two coral gardens, moving wherever the waterway was clear. Behind a curtain of silver fish, she found the turtle resting safely inside a giant shell.

The friends did not pull or frighten it. They formed a gentle path of glowing pearls. The turtle followed the lights back to the lagoon just as the tide began to flow towards home.

“Being brave does not always mean moving first,” Nerina said. “Sometimes it means looking until the right path appears.” The Coral Court celebrated with shell music, and the little turtle slept beside its family. On the reef chessboard, the players remembered the quiet current: before you move, notice where every path leads.

## The story of Sweet Fairy: The Seed That Would Not Hurry

In Honeyblossom Glade, one moonflower seed refused to sprout. Queen Faye Honeyblossom tried a sparkle spell, a warm-rain spell and a cheerful song. Nothing happened.

King Bram Bramblecoat knelt beside the soil. “Perhaps it needs a plan, not more magic,” he said. Mallow Quill flew along diagonal garden paths to check the shade. Acorn Hall kept the straight irrigation channels clear. Pip Thistledown jumped over two rows of thyme and one row of mint to fetch rich compost without crushing a single shoot.

The Dewdrop Sprites carried water forward in tiny cups. They could not step backwards, so each sprite checked the way ahead before moving. Faye realised that the moonflower needed darkness as well as care. Everyone had been shining too much light upon it.

They built a small leaf screen and waited. Waiting felt harder than casting a spell. By nightfall, a silver stem curled out of the earth. The flower opened beneath the moon, wide enough for the whole court to shelter under its petals.

Faye laughed softly. “The strongest move is not always the biggest move.” From then on, the fairies played chess beside the moonflower. They developed their knights and bishops, protected one another and let good positions grow. The flower never hurried, but it always opened at exactly the right time.

## The story of the Starlight Alliance: The Beacon Beyond the Comet

The Starlight Alliance received a faint signal from a tiny research pod beyond a comet field. Captain Orion Beacon marked the pod's position and asked every crew member for an idea.

Dr Quasar mapped long diagonal gaps between the comets. Starbase Seven opened a straight launch corridor. Astrohoof, the robot space-horse, could jump in an L-shaped route around obstacles that blocked everyone else. The Pocket Rovers rolled forward to place bright guide beacons, one safe space at a time.

Engineer-Princess Nova Swift studied the whole map. A direct flight looked quick, but it would leave the rescue ship unprotected. She moved the team into useful positions first. Quasar guarded one diagonal. Starbase Seven guarded a file. Astrohoof hopped to a square no comet could strike.

Only then did Nova launch. The fleet reached the research pod, repaired its bent antenna and shared enough power for the journey home. Nobody boasted. The rescued explorers had also been brave: they had conserved air, sent a clear signal and waited for help.

Back at the starbase, Orion placed the final beacon on their chessboard. “Exploring is not guessing,” he said. “We look ahead, protect our team and keep a safe way home.” The alliance began a new game while the comet field sparkled harmlessly outside the window.

## The story of the Sunlit System: The Moon Map

King Sol shone at the centre of the Sunlit System, while the planets travelled around the Sun in their own paths. Queen Earth had one Moon, Mars had two small moons, and giant Jupiter had many. Each world was different, and none needed to pretend to be another.

One day, a Moon Messenger found an old map with one orbit rubbed away. Earth gathered the nobles. Saturn tilted its true rings towards the light and spotted a faint diagonal scratch. Jupiter Stormkeep watched a broad straight path across the chart. Mars sent the comet-shaped knight around the edge in a surprising L-shaped leap.

The Moon Messengers carried careful observations forward. Sol did not command the planets to change their paths; the Sun's gravity already kept the system together. Instead, everyone compared what they knew. The missing line belonged to a small moon, not a planet.

Earth painted the orbit back onto the map. It curved around its planet, exactly where the observations showed. The messenger beamed. Being small had not made its journey unimportant.

“A true map respects every traveller,” Earth said. “A good chess plan does too.” On their board, the grand pieces made room for the pawns, and the pawns protected the grand pieces. Above them, planets continued orbiting the Sun—not in a neat row, but across the vast, real depth of space.

## Meet the board

A chessboard has 64 squares: 32 light and 32 dark. Set the board so a light square is at each player's right-hand corner. Eight vertical lines are called files. Eight horizontal lines are called ranks. Long sloping lines are diagonals.

Dawn sets up on ranks 1 and 2 and moves first. Dusk sets up on ranks 8 and 7. From left to right, the back row is rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook. The queen begins on her own colour: the light queen on a light square and the dark queen on a dark square. Eight pawns stand in the row ahead.

## How the pieces move

### King

The king moves one square in any direction: straight or diagonal. A king may never move into check. The two kings may not stand next to one another because each would attack the other.

### Queen

The queen slides any number of clear squares straight or diagonal. She cannot jump over a piece. The queen is powerful, so protect her and do not send her out alone too early.

### Rook

The rook slides any number of clear squares forwards, backwards or sideways. It cannot move diagonally and cannot jump. Two connected rooks can guard one another strongly.

### Bishop

The bishop slides any number of clear squares diagonally. It cannot jump. A bishop that begins on a light square always stays on light squares; one that begins on a dark square always stays on dark squares.

### Knight

The knight moves in an L: two squares in one straight direction, then one square sideways. It is the only piece that can jump over other pieces. A knight always lands on the opposite square colour from where it began.

### Pawn

A pawn moves straight forward one square into an empty square. From its starting square, it may move forward two empty squares. A pawn captures one square diagonally forward; it never captures straight ahead and never moves backwards.

## Capturing

Most pieces capture by moving onto a square occupied by an opponent's piece. The captured piece leaves the board. You may not capture your own piece. No piece except a knight can move through an occupied square.

## Check and checkmate

**Check** means the king is attacked. The player in check must make the king safe immediately by moving the king, capturing the attacking piece, or blocking the attack when blocking is possible.

**Checkmate** means the king is in check and no legal move can make it safe. The game ends, and the player who gave checkmate wins. The king is never captured.

**Stalemate** happens when a player is not in check but has no legal move. Stalemate is a draw.

## Special moves

### Castling

Castling is one king move that also moves a rook. The king moves two squares towards the rook, then that rook moves to the square the king crossed. You may castle only when the king and that rook have not moved, the squares between them are empty, the king is not in check, and the king does not cross or land on an attacked square.

### Promotion

When a pawn reaches the farthest rank, it must become a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same side. It usually becomes a queen. The new piece appears immediately, even if another piece of that type is still on the board.

### En passant: the secret pawn move

If an opposing pawn moves two squares from its starting square and lands beside your pawn, your pawn may capture it as though it had moved only one square. Your pawn moves diagonally into the square the other pawn crossed, and the other pawn leaves the board. En passant is allowed only on the very next move.

## Eight clever chess tips

1. **Control the centre.** Pieces in or aimed towards the four central squares can reach more of the board.
2. **Develop knights and bishops.** Bring these pieces away from the back row early so they can help.
3. **Castle early.** Castling usually moves your king towards safety and brings a rook into play.
4. **Do not rush the queen out.** Smaller pieces may chase her and gain useful moves.
5. **Ask, “What does my opponent want?”** Look for checks, captures and threats before choosing your move.
6. **Protect your pieces.** Try not to leave a piece where it can be taken for free.
7. **Look for checks, captures and threats.** Check these forcing ideas for both players on every turn.
8. **Use all your team.** A plan with several active pieces is stronger than one lonely attack.

## A calm turn routine

Before touching a piece, ask:

1. Is my king safe?
2. What did my opponent's last move change?
3. Do I have a useful check, capture or threat?
4. After my move, what can my opponent capture?

Then choose and play. If your idea does not work, study the position. The board has taught you something useful.

## Glossary

- **Attack:** To threaten to capture a piece on the next move.
- **Capture:** To move onto an opponent's piece and remove it from the board.
- **Castle:** The special move that moves the king two squares and a rook across it.
- **Check:** An attack on the king that must be answered at once.
- **Checkmate:** Check with no legal way to make the king safe; the game ends.
- **Colourway:** The light Dawn or dark Dusk colours used by a kingdom's pieces.
- **Diagonal:** A sloping line of squares of the same colour.
- **Draw:** A game with no winner, including stalemate and other official draw conditions.
- **En passant:** A special immediate pawn capture after an opposing pawn moves two squares beside it.
- **File:** A vertical line of eight squares.
- **Legal move:** A move allowed by every chess rule and which does not leave your king in check.
- **Opponent:** The player on the other side of the board.
- **Promote:** To replace a pawn that reaches the farthest rank with a queen, rook, bishop or knight.
- **Rank:** A horizontal line of eight squares.
- **Stalemate:** A draw in which the player to move is not in check but has no legal move.
- **Square:** One light or dark space on the chessboard.
