Color Lab: new colour words

Dot read colour words across dictionaries, books, fashion, beauty and art, and found about 180 real names Color Lab does not have yet. Our own count of 22 classic children's books agrees: most book colour words are already on the shelf, or are another name for a shade we already show. Five questions below. Just reply in chat, for example "1 yes, 2 A, 3 later, 4 skip, 5 yes".

1. "Also called" words on cards recommended

Many words a child reads are the same shade as a card we already have. Instead of a new, near-identical card, the grown card says "Also called …", so the child meets the book word next to the colour.

black ← jet
slate grey ← slate
straw yellow ← straw
cherry red ← cherry
burnt orange ← ochre
camel ← tawny

Yes, or no?

About 30 words from the books, plus Dot's short forms (dove → dove grey, chartreuse, snow white). Each pairing is checked by eye, not only by maths. Every added word is one short voice line.

2. Which new cards?

Only words whose shade is clearly different from every card we have (same spacing rule as before).

A. Book words first recommended

bay (horse colour)
ebony
alabaster
thistle

B. A + Dot's strongest everyday words (each one checked for spacing first; some will turn into "also called")

garnet, oxblood, rosewood, malachite, electric blue, rose gold, pewter, graphite, dove grey, greige, biscuit, oatmeal, jet black

Left out on purpose: claret, champagne, Bordeaux, wine (alcohol); livid, sallow, ashen, pallid, wan (illness or injury); bridal whites (question 4).

3. "How it looks" words: shiny, glittery, metallic, see-through

Stories use these a lot (glittering, shimmering, pearly, dull). Dot points out they are not colours, so one flat card cannot show them.

Later, as its own small game recommended · or skip

Words: glittery, shiny, sparkly, metallic, shimmery, pearly, matte, see-through.

4. Wedding-dress whites

Dot found 30 bridal names: diamond white, silk white, oyster, candlelight, light ivory and more. They differ by shop, with no shared scale.

Skip for this age recommended · or add a few

5. Hair colours as their own collection recommended

Dot's newer idea (31 Oct): hair colour words come up in almost every story, so give them a proper shelf of drawn hair locks, each with a short line that says how it differs.

jet black · blue-black · raven
chestnut · chocolate · mocha · brunette
golden · honey · ash · platinum · strawberry blonde
ginger · copper · auburn
silver · salt-and-pepper

Examples of the explaining lines: "Strawberry blonde is blonde with a little red, not strawberry-red." "Salt-and-pepper is dark and grey hairs mixed together." "Dirty blonde means a darker blonde, not dirty hair."

Yes, or no? If yes: inside Explore, or its own shelf?

Highlights, ombré and balayage would be a separate "how it's done" part, since they arrange colours rather than name one. Needs drawn hair locks (an art job) and voice lines, so it would start as a small pilot.

Fixing now, no decision needed

  • 237 cards had the same few examples pasted on many cards, so ruby said "a seashell" and topaz said "the daytime sky". Every card gets its own true examples; the comparison line is checked against the real colours.
  • Topaz on our shelf is blue-green, but in books topaz is usually golden brown. Its card will say "blue topaz" in the examples.
