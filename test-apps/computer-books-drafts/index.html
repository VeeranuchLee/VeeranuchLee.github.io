What Does a Computer Do?

many-jobs

A computer can show pictures, play songs, and help you write.

Quick words picture — รูปภาพ (easy) · song — เพลง (easy) · computer — คอมพิวเตอร์ (key)

A computer takes in what you do. Then it follows its instructions and shows you an answer.

Quick words take in — รับเข้า (easy) · instruction — คำสั่ง (key) · answer — คำตอบ (easy)

input

Taps, typing, and speaking give the computer information. This is called input.

Quick words tap — แตะ (easy) · type — พิมพ์ (easy) · input — ข้อมูลเข้า (key, check Thai)

instructions-inside

Inside the computer is a long list of instructions. People wrote that list.

Quick words list — รายการ (easy) · people — ผู้คน (easy) · instruction — คำสั่ง (key, deliberate return)

output

The answer that comes out is called output. It can be a picture, a sound, or words.

Quick words picture — รูปภาพ (easy) · sound — เสียง (easy) · output — ผลลัพธ์ (key, check Thai)

very-fast

A computer follows each little step very fast. It can do millions of steps in one second.

Quick words fast — เร็ว (easy) · step — ขั้นตอน (easy) · million — ล้าน (key)

exactly

A computer does what its instructions say. It does not do what you meant to say.

Quick words follow — ทำตาม (easy) · word — คำ (easy) · exactly — อย่างแม่นยำ (key, check Thai)

computers-inside

Small computers live inside other machines. A washing machine has one, and so does a traffic light.

Quick words washing machine — เครื่องซักผ้า (easy) · traffic light — ไฟจราจร (easy) · inside — ข้างใน (key)

closing

A computer is a fast helper that follows instructions. How many machines with a screen can you count in your home?

Quick words screen — หน้าจอ (key) · count — นับ (easy) · home — บ้าน (easy)

Notes for grown-ups

Computer & Digital World series, first wave, drafted 2026-10-07. Section: Using Computers. Structure overview: ../STRUCTURE.md. Task record: ../../../../coordination/tasks/2026-10-07-0900-glm-computers-wave1-texts.md. 10 screens: cover + 8 concept pages + closing. American spelling (color, favorite).

Core idea: the series' spine, stated plainly — a computer takes in what you do, follows a list of instructions people wrote, and gives you an answer, very fast. It does exactly what the instructions say. This book opens the whole series; every later book is one of its doors (data, instructions, the internet, privacy, AI).

Child text rules applied here: 1–2 short sentences per page, words a 7-year-old reads with help; no hedging words; no brand names ("computer", "tablet", "game machine"); no machine that wants, thinks or guesses.

cover

Goal. welcome; the title carries the question the book answers.

Art. the series cast in the rain books' watercolour style — the older girl at a small home desk with a plain generic desktop computer (monitor, keyboard, no logos), the younger boy leaning on her chair, Vanilla the small papillon (standing about knee-height, 1/4–1/3 of the girl's height) with front paws on the boy's stool, all watching the screen with mild curiosity. Warm room, plants, afternoon light. No text in the art.

many-jobs

Goal. start from what the child already knows a computer does.

Art. a plain desk computer at the center; three soft vignettes around it connected by gentle arrows: a photo of a flower, a singing bird with music notes, a hand writing a story. One machine, three different jobs. No logos.

Facts. — (familiar ground; the book's own frame).

three-steps

Goal. the spine itself — in, follow instructions, out.

Art. a left-to-right three-panel diagram in soft watercolor: (1) a child's hand tapping a tablet, (2) the computer's case drawn open with a scroll of numbered steps inside, (3) a screen showing a colorful picture. Three arrows connect the panels in order. App-added labels: "in", "instructions", "out". The scroll-of-steps inside the case is the picture the rest of the series reuses.

Facts. Input → processing → output is the standard model of what a computer does. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing

input

Goal. name the "in" step: input.

Art. one computer at the right edge; three arrows flowing into it from a tapping finger, a keyboard with dancing letters, and an open mouth with speech marks. All arrows point the same way — in. App-added label: "input — ข้อมูลเข้า" assembled by the app, not painted.

Facts. Input devices send information to the computer; output devices give answers out. The Thai Quick Word ข้อมูลเข้า is the standard textbook term for input and carries a (check) for the owner's ear. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input_device

instructions-inside

Goal. the honest heart of the book — the instructions are a list people wrote.

Art. the open computer case again, the step-scroll unrolled further this time so numbered steps are visibly written lines; beside the computer, a friendly adult and the older girl looking at a paper of steps together (hands and faces allowed here — the page's point is that people wrote the list). No logos, no real code.

Facts. A program is a list of instructions written by people; a computer carries out (executes) those instructions. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program

output

Goal. name the "out" step: output.

Art. the mirror of input: arrows flowing OUT of one computer toward a screen showing a colorful drawing, a speaker with music notes, and a printed page of large friendly words. App-added label: "output".

Facts. ผลลัพธ์ is the standard Thai term for output/result; kept identical wherever the series says output. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input/output

very-fast

Goal. the speed that makes computers useful — and the reason exactness matters.

Art. a race-track lane drawn as a loop of tiny numbered footprints (steps) circling through the computer case; a stopwatch at the corner showing one second; the footprints blur into a ribbon where they speed up. Satisfying, not frantic.

Facts. Modern processors execute on the order of billions of instructions per second; the child text says "millions" as a deliberately conservative, still-true floor. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instructions_per_second

exactly

Goal. the sentence Book 3 grows from — the computer does what is said, not what is meant.

Art. two panels. LEFT: a speech bubble with a neat numbered list; the computer happily drawing exactly the square house the list describes. RIGHT: the boy waving his hands with a vague speech bubble (a scribble); the computer drawing a wobbly triangle, still happily, because that is what the scribble said. Calm and funny, no failure.

Facts. Computers execute instructions as written (the basis of Book 3's exactness game). Deliberately avoids "the computer guesses" — guessing belongs to the AI book, where it is defined properly. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithm (an algorithm is a precisely defined sequence of steps).

computers-inside

Goal. small computers live inside everyday machines — the shelf's "Computers Are Everywhere" book is previewed, not spent.

Facts. Appliances and signals are run by embedded computers (small computers inside other devices). SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_system

closing

Goal. house closing convention; hand back to the child with a look-around question.

Art. the cast on the sofa: the girl with a tablet, the boy pointing at the TV across the room, Vanilla asleep across their laps; a lamp, a microwave and a wall clock with friendly faces in the background waiting to be counted. Cozy end-of-day light.

what-is-a-file

Goal. name the thing that was saved: a file.

Art. one large friendly file drawn as a paper sheet with a folded corner and a name-tag (squiggle, unreadable); inside its outline, a watercolor drawing, a song note and a story page — showing that pictures, songs and stories all become files. A dotted line counts them: one file, one thing.

Facts. A computer file is a named unit of information kept in storage — pictures, songs and text are all stored as files. Thai Quick Word uses everyday ไฟล์; the formal Thai term แฟ้มข้อมูล is noted for the owner. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_file

file-names

Goal. files have names; good names tell you what is inside.

The boy picks the dog one with a smile. Tags are glyphs, not readable words — art carries no text.

Facts. — (naming habit; no external claim).

folders

Art. a cutaway of one folder drawn as a soft cardboard box holding three file-sheets upright like letters; beside it a bigger box holds two smaller boxes — nesting shown, not narrated. Labels as glyphs only. Vanilla sits beside the small box, the same height as it, for scale.

Facts. Folders (directories) group files; a directory can contain other directories. Thai Quick Word uses everyday โฟลเดอร์; formal Thai is แฟ้ม — flagged (check) for the owner's ear. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directory_(computing)

storage

Goal. where the boxes live: storage; it keeps files with the power off.

Facts. Non-volatile storage (the tablet's built-in storage) retains data with no power. Thai ที่เก็บข้อมูล is a plain-language coinage for this book — flagged (check); the technical Thai term is หน่วยเก็บข้อมูล. SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_data_storage
